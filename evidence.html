📚 Reference

Evidence Log

Every mechanic in Flight Deck, the study it came from, and where it's actually used. Where the evidence is weaker than the popular version of the claim, it says so.
How to read the strength labels. Strong means a meta-analysis or multiple randomised trials. Good means at least one well-powered randomised or controlled study. Suggestive means real evidence exists but it's limited, single-study, or contested. Nothing here is labelled strong on the basis of one paper.
Honesty section

What this system deliberately leaves out

A productivity system that only cites the findings that support it isn't evidence-based, it's decorated. These are ideas you'll find in almost every self-improvement app, left out on purpose.

Ego depletion / “willpower is a finite battery”

The original glucose-and-willpower model has failed to replicate in large pre-registered multi-lab attempts. Nothing in Flight Deck is built on “conserve your willpower.” The system instead assumes willpower is unreliable at any hour, which is why it leans on environment design and pre-commitment.

Learning styles (visual / auditory / kinaesthetic)

Repeatedly tested, repeatedly unsupported. Matching instruction to a claimed style does not improve learning. There is no “what kind of learner are you” step in this app for that reason.

“It takes 21 days to build a habit”

This comes from a 1960 surgeon's observation about patients adjusting to amputations, not a study of habits. The measured figure is a median of 66 days with enormous individual variation, which is what the automaticity bars in the Habit Reactor are scaled to.

“Dopamine detox”

Not a neuroscientific concept. Dopamine isn't a pleasure reservoir you drain and refill. The Firewall module works on urge tolerance and environment, which have actual trial data behind them, rather than on any claim about receptor levels.

Grit as a distinct predictor

A meta-analysis found grit correlates highly with conscientiousness and adds little predictive power for performance beyond it. Nothing here asks you to rate your grit, and the system is designed so that consistency comes from structure rather than from a personality trait you either have or don't.

The Pomodoro interval specifically

Timeboxing works, but 25 minutes has no special empirical status — it was one man's kitchen timer. The evidence supports breaks and bounded sessions, not that number. That's why Study Flight offers 25, 50 and 90 minutes and doesn't claim one is correct.

Design note

On making it addictive

You asked for addictive, and the streaks, XP, levels, variable-ratio bonuses and completion rings here are taken straight from the mechanics that make consumer apps hard to put down. They work, and they're pointed at things worth doing.

Three deliberate limits, so the mechanics stay in service of the goal: there's no infinite feed and nothing to scroll for its own sake; you earn a streak freeze every seven days so a single missed day can't trigger the all-or-nothing collapse that usually ends these attempts; and the app never notifies you or asks for your attention — you come to it, at two times you chose.

If you ever notice you're optimising the XP number rather than the thing the XP is standing in for, that's the signal to ignore the score for a week. The number is scaffolding. It's supposed to become unnecessary.

Reference list is abbreviated. Author, year, journal, volume and pages are given so you can find each paper directly. Where a finding is summarised, it reflects the paper's primary reported result — go to the source before you rely on any of it for anything that matters.
