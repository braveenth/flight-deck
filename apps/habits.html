The number that matters

66 days, not 21

The “21 days to a habit” line has no research behind it. When it was actually measured — 96 people, one new daily behaviour each, tracked to automaticity — the median was 66 days, and the range ran from 18 to 254. Simple behaviours (drinking water) automated fast; complex ones (50 sit-ups) took far longer.

The same study found that missing a single day had no measurable effect on the eventual automaticity curve. That's the entire justification for the never-miss-twice rule — and for the streak freeze this app gives you every seven days.
Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010), European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6):998–1009.
Design rule

Shrink until it's stupid

If you've missed a habit three times, it's too big. Cut it in half. A habit you do badly every day beats a habit you do perfectly twice a month, because you're training the trigger, not the performance.

Ten pages, not a chapter. Two minutes, not thirty. Raise the bar only after the trigger fires by itself.
