Setting

When your day rolls over

Your day ends when you sleep, not at midnight. Work and a Debrief done before this hour count toward the day that just ended.

Set it to midnight to turn the behaviour off entirely. Changing it re-attributes future entries only — nothing already logged moves.
Why this matters

Timing is a variable you control

Consistency beats optimisation. The gap between when you sleep on free days and on scheduled days — "social jetlag" — is associated with worse health and mood outcomes independent of how much sleep you get. A schedule that holds is worth more than a schedule that's theoretically ideal.
Wittmann, Dinich, Merrow & Roenneberg (2006), Chronobiology International 23(1–2):497–509.
Cognitive performance varies across the day, and the direction depends on your chronotype and the task — there's no universally correct hour to study. The hour histogram above is how you find your answer rather than adopting someone else's.
Schmidt, Collette, Cajochen & Peigneux (2007), Cognitive Neuropsychology 24(7):755–789.
Note that this page shows when you worked, not how well. Cross-reference it with day ratings in Flight Recorder before you conclude a particular hour is your best one.
