Sleep

Log this in the morning. Consistency of timing matters at least as much as duration.

Sleep is not downtime for memory — it's the processing step. Consolidation during sleep is what turns today's fragile encoding into something you'll still have next month. Cutting it is the most expensive "extra study hour" available.
Rasch & Born (2013), Physiological Reviews 93(2):681–766 — "About sleep's role in memory."
Across ~60,000 people wearing accelerometers, how regular sleep timing was predicted mortality risk more strongly than how long people slept. Same bedtime beats more hours.
Windred et al. (2024), Sleep 47(1):zsad253.

Training

Aerobic exercise physically enlarges the hippocampus. In a one-year randomised trial of 120 older adults, moderate aerobic walking increased anterior hippocampal volume by ~2% — effectively reversing one to two years of age-related loss — and the increase tracked with improved spatial memory. The stretching control group declined.
Erickson et al. (2011), PNAS 108(7):3017–3022.

Screens before bed

In a controlled inpatient study, reading on a light-emitting device for 4 hours before bed suppressed melatonin, delayed circadian timing by roughly 1.5 hours, took longer to fall asleep, and left people measurably less alert the next morning — compared to reading a print book.

Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler (2015), PNAS 112(4):1232–1237.
