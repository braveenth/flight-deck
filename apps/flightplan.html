Why numbers

Specific and hard beats “do your best”

Across four decades and hundreds of studies, specific and difficult goals produced consistently higher performance than vague ones or urging people to try hard — as long as the person is committed to the goal and gets feedback on progress. Both of those conditions are what the rest of this app exists to supply.

Locke & Latham (2002), American Psychologist 57(9):705–717.

Rule of three

Why you can't have six goals

Every additional goal divides the same finite attention and makes each one's daily process target smaller and more skippable. Three is the working ceiling; two is better. Anything you cut now you can start on day 121.
