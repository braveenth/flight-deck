🌅 Daily · Morning · ~4 minutes

Preflight

You are not deciding what to do today. You are removing the decisions before they can cost you anything.
Step 1 of 6 · Wake protocol

Set the machine before you set the plan.

Do these before you look at a feed. Morning outdoor light is the strongest signal your circadian clock takes, and it sets tonight's sleep as much as this morning's alertness.

Wright et al. (2013), Current Biology 23(16):1554–1558. A week of natural light exposure advanced circadian timing by ~2 hours and locked melatonin onset to sunset — indoor-only light did not.
Step 2 of 6 · Identity

Who are you being today?

Goals framed as identity survive bad days better than goals framed as tasks. You are not trying to study today. You are being the kind of person who studies, and today is just evidence.

Make it a verb you can be caught doing. “Someone who is disciplined” is not checkable. “Someone who opens the book before the phone” is.
Oyserman, Bybee & Terry (2006), JPSP 91(1):188–204. An intervention linking academic effort to students’ possible future selves improved grades, attendance and behaviour over two years.
Step 3 of 6 · Targets

Three outcomes. Not a task list.

Specific and hard beats vague and easy — but only when the goal is something you can actually finish today. Write outcomes you could hold up at 9pm and say “that happened” or “it didn’t.”

Locke & Latham (2002), American Psychologist 57(9):705–717. 35 years of research: specific, difficult goals produce higher performance than “do your best,” across 100+ tasks.
Step 4 of 6 · WOOP

Name the obstacle before it names you.

Positive visualisation alone slightly reduces effort. Contrasting the wish against the real internal obstacle, then pre-loading an if-then response, is the version that works.

Then
Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38:69–119. Meta-analysis of 94 studies, 8,461 participants: if-then implementation intentions had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65) over goal intentions alone. · Oettingen (2012), European Review of Social Psychology 23(1):1–63 for mental contrasting.
Step 5 of 6 · When and where

An unscheduled goal is a wish.

The single biggest predictor of whether these three happen is whether they have a time and a place attached right now. Be concrete: a clock time and a physical location.

Step 6 of 6 · Commit

Lock the flight plan.

Once you commit, this becomes the day Mission Control and tonight’s Debrief score you against. You can change it — but you have to change it deliberately, which is the whole point.
